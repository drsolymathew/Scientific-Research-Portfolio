Academic Background

Materials Innovation Grounded in Experimental Rigour

Dr. Soly Mathew is a materials scientist and research scholar in the Department of Physics at Vellore Institute of Technology (VIT), India. Her research focuses on the molecular and structural engineering of flexible polymer nanocomposites designed for high-sensitivity capacitive pressure transduction.

Her doctoral work addresses key bottlenecks in flexible electronics: overcoming the trade-off between elastic compliance and dielectric permittivity in elastomeric materials. By synthesizing ternary dielectric composites—incorporating high-k ceramic nanoparticles (TiO₂, ZnO, ZrO₂, La₂O₃) and conductive nanoscale fillers (graphene oxide, carbon black) into polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) and poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) matrices—she has successfully engineered thin-film sensor architectures with expanded linear sensitivity domains and fast viscoelastic recovery kinetics.

With 5 verified first-author publications in leading peer-reviewed journals including Smart Materials and Structures, Journal of Applied Polymer Science, Flexible and Printed Electronics, Sensors International, and IEEE Access, her work bridges basic solid-state physics with scalable device microfabrication.

Her academic career includes serving as a Project Associate at the Indian Institute of Technology Jodhpur (IIT Jodhpur) (Oct 2025 – Jun 2026), conducting advanced materials and sensor R&D at a premier national institute of technology. Prior to that, she served as an Assistant Professor (Adhoc) at NSS College of Engineering, Palakkad (Aug 2025 – Oct 2025), lecturing in engineering physics, supervising laboratory sessions, and mentoring undergraduate engineers in materials science fundamentals.

Scientific Specialisations

Core Research Competencies

Systematic experimental workflows from nanoparticle synthesis and dielectric composite formulation to microfabrication and high-cycle electromechanical validation.

Functional Nanocomposite Materials

Engineering multi-phase dielectric elastomer composites (DECs) loaded with ceramic nanoparticles (TiO₂, ZnO, ZrO₂, La₂O₃) and carbonaceous conductors (graphene oxide, carbon black). Optimizing filler dispersion and surface functionalization to prevent agglomeration.

Key Methodologies
Ultrasonic Colloid Milling Percolation Control Interfacial Polarization

Flexible Capacitive Pressure Sensors

Design and assembly of parallel-plate capacitive pressure sensor (CPS) devices featuring micro-structured and thin-film dielectric layers. Maximizing relative capacitance sensitivity (ΔC/C₀) while mitigating viscoelastic hysteresis under low-pressure regimens.

Key Methodologies
Parallel Plate Architecture Sub-kPa Detection Low-Noise Readout

Polymer Thin-Film Fabrication

Precision spin-coating, solution casting, and thermal curing of polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) and poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) thin films. Precise thickness control (~50–200 µm) and cleanroom vacuum degassing to eliminate micro-voids.

Key Methodologies
Spin Coating (1000–3500 rpm) Thermal Crosslinking Degassing

Materials Characterisation

Comprehensive physical, chemical, and morphological structural evaluation using Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FESEM), Energy Dispersive X-ray (EDX), X-ray Diffraction (XRD), and Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR).

Key Methodologies
FESEM & Morphology XRD Phase Analysis FTIR Chemical Bonding

Experimental Sensor Evaluation

Electromechanical characterisation via precision motorized compression testing rigs coupled to high-frequency LCR meters. Quantitative determination of working pressure range, sensitivity (S), hysteresis error, response/relaxation times, and 2,000+ cycle fatigue life.

Key Methodologies
High-Frequency LCR Testing Compressive Stress-Strain Hysteresis Quantitation

Wearable Sensing Applications

Experimental translation of flexible pressure sensors to human physiological pulse monitoring (radial artery percussion, tidal, and dicrotic waves), finger/joint articulation tracking, and low-pressure tactile interfaces for biomedical diagnostics and soft robotics.

Key Methodologies
Radial Artery Pulse Waves Sub-50ms Response Soft Skin Compatibility
