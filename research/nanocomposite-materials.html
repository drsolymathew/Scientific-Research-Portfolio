PDMS–CB–ZnO Ternary Nanocomposites: Interfacial Polarization & Controlled Percolation

Harnessing conductive carbon black networks and semiconductor ZnO nanostructures to maximize effective dielectric permittivity without causing dielectric breakdown.

Primary Author Dr. Soly Mathew (VIT)
Supervisor / Co-Author Dr. Krishnamoorthi Chintagumpala
Journal • Publisher Flex. Print. Electron. • IOP Publishing
DOI Identifier 10.1088/2058-8585/adc81e

1. Scientific Research Problem

In flexible capacitive sensor design, increasing the dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_r$) of the elastomer sandwich layer directly translates into higher baseline capacitance ($C_0$) and greater sensitivity ($\Delta C / C_0$). One classic approach involves incorporating conductive fillers like carbon black (CB) or graphene to form micro-capacitive networks.

However, conductive nanofillers present an acute percolation threshold risk. Near the percolation threshold ($f_c$), dielectric constant surges sharply, but electrical conductivity rises by orders of magnitude simultaneously, causing intolerable dielectric leakage currents, high dissipation factors ($\tan \delta$), and irreversible short-circuiting under mechanical compression.

2. Research Objectives

  • Establish a dual-filler ternary strategy combining sub-percolative conductive carbon black (CB) with semiconducting zinc oxide (ZnO) inside a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) matrix.
  • Prevent ohmic current conduction by using semiconductor ZnO boundaries that act as dielectric barriers between adjacent conductive CB clusters.
  • Investigate the resulting broadband dielectric permittivity and electromechanical cyclic response across the tactile pressure spectrum.

3. Materials & Formulation Chemistry

Conductive carbon black (particle size ~30 nm, high specific surface area) was introduced at concentrations strictly maintained below the empirical percolation threshold (≤ 0.4 wt%). Nanoscale zinc oxide (ZnO) was incorporated at 0.5 wt% to act as a polarizable buffer.

4. Experimental Methodology

  1. Surface Treatment: Nanoparticle surfaces were treated with silane coupling agents to improve interfacial adhesion with the hydrophobic silicone polymer backbone.
  2. Two-Step Ultrasonic Compounding: ZnO particles were first blended into liquid PDMS prepolymer, followed by incremental carbon black introduction under intermittent pulse sonication to prevent premature conductive path bridging.
  3. Doctor-Blade Thin-Film Casting: The degassed mixture was cast using an automated precision doctor-blade coater onto polyimide release films to ensure uniform layer thickness (~120 µm).
  4. Impedance Spectroscopy: Broadband dielectric measurements were conducted from 100 Hz to 1 MHz using a high-precision impedance analyzer to record permittivity and AC conductivity.

5. Documented Research Findings

  • Synergistic Permittivity Surge: The ternary PDMS-CB-ZnO composite exhibited an effective dielectric constant of $\varepsilon_r \approx 10.45$ at 1 kHz—an increase of over 3.8-fold relative to pristine PDMS—while keeping the loss tangent below 0.03.
  • Micro-Capacitor Network Formation: The presence of semi-insulating ZnO nanorods between neighboring carbon black domains created localized boundary layers that impeded electron hopping while intensifying charge accumulation.
  • Negligible Hysteresis: Mechanical compression tests up to 5 kPa demonstrated very low hysteresis error (< 4%) and consistent baseline capacitance recovery across repeated load-unload cycles.

6. Scientific Limitations

Precise processing control is paramount: variations in sonication time or temperature during mixing can alter the local carbon black cluster morphology, shifting the percolation threshold and increasing sample-to-sample variance. Industrial scale-up requires closed-loop rheological monitoring.

7. Industrial Relevance

Ternary dielectric formulations provide a generalizable paradigm for high-permittivity flexible elastomer design. Beyond pressure sensing, this architecture has direct applicability in flexible electroactive polymer (EAP) actuators, energy harvesting dielectric generators, and capacitive touch screens.
