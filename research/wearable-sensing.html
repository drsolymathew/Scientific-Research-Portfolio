Wearable Capacitive Sensors with ZnO–GO–PVDF Dielectric Composites

Engineering synergistic 2D graphene oxide and zinc oxide nanostructures in poly(vinylidene fluoride) for non-invasive cardiovascular pulse waveform resolution and subtle tactile feedback.

Primary Author Dr. Soly Mathew (VIT)
Supervisor / Co-Author Dr. Krishnamoorthi Chintagumpala
Journal • Publisher J. Appl. Polym. Sci. • Wiley
DOI Identifier 10.1002/app.70439

1. Scientific Research Problem

Non-invasive continuous physiological monitoring requires epidermal pressure sensors that are ultra-thin, mechanically compliant, and capable of detecting fractional pressure variations below 500 Pa. For instance, recording the subtle tactile pressure pulsations of the human radial artery requires resolving distinct anatomical peaks: the percussion wave (P1, ventricular ejection), tidal wave (P2, reflected systolic wave), and dicrotic notch (P3, aortic valve closure).

Standard elastomeric sensors often exhibit sluggish viscoelastic relaxation (>100 ms), which blurs high-frequency hemodynamic waveforms into indistinct pulses. Moreover, pure polar polymers such as poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) have moderate dielectric constants (εr ≈ 8–10), demanding innovative nanofiller architectures to maximize capacitive transduction sensitivity.

2. Research Objectives

  • Synthesize a synergistic composite dielectric layer embedding 2D conductive graphene oxide (GO) nanosheets and semiconductor zinc oxide (ZnO) nanoparticles within a flexible PVDF polymer matrix.
  • Optimize interfacial electric field distribution and polarization to achieve high capacitive sensitivity in the acoustic and physiological pressure regimes (< 2 kPa).
  • Experimentally evaluate wrist pulse wave resolution and tactile flexion response using real-time high-speed LCR acquisition.

3. Materials & Formulation Chemistry

PVDF pellets were dissolved in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) / acetone co-solvents under magnetic stirring at 60°C. Graphene oxide (GO) nanosheets (~1–2 atomic layers) prepared via modified Hummers' method and ZnO nanoparticles were co-introduced in controlled stoichiometric ratios.

4. Experimental Methodology

  1. Colloid Dispersion: GO sheets were exfoliated in DMF via bath sonication for 2 hours, after which ZnO nanoparticles were added and probe sonicated to create decorated ZnO@GO hybrid structures.
  2. Solution Blending: The hybrid dispersion was compounded into dissolved PVDF and homogenized at 70°C.
  3. Film Casting & Phase Inversion: The solution was cast onto glass substrates using an automatic film applicator, followed by controlled thermal evaporation at 90°C to induce polar β-phase nucleation.
  4. Sensor Packaging: Thin flexible copper electrodes and polyimide encapsulation were applied to formulate an epidermal patch (~80 µm total thickness).
  5. Physiological Testing: Sensor patches were positioned directly above the radial artery of healthy volunteers with non-invasive medical tape to capture continuous real-time waveforms.

5. Documented Research Findings

  • High-Fidelity Waveform Resolution: The fabricated sensor cleanly resolved the P1 percussion wave, P2 tidal wave, and P3 dicrotic notch, enabling calculation of arterial stiffness indices such as the radial augmentation index (AIr = P2/P1).
  • Rapid Response Kinetics: Step-loading experiments revealed an instantaneous response time of 38 ms and recovery time of 45 ms, easily tracking human heart rates (60–100 bpm) without signal attenuation or baseline wandering.
  • Tactile Gesture Tracking: Mounting sensors on finger joints successfully demonstrated repeatable capacitive spikes corresponding to subtle flexion angles (15° to 90°).

6. Scientific Limitations

Skin-sensor interface stability remains subject to motion artifacts if the patch is subjected to lateral shear forces. Current demonstrations were conducted in controlled laboratory settings; deployment in unconstrained mobile environments will require specialized differential noise-cancelling electrode layouts.

7. Industrial Relevance

This technology opens scalable avenues for continuous cardiovascular telemedicine, post-operative monitoring, and responsive prosthetic tactile feedback without requiring cumbersome pneumatic cuffs or rigid transducer housings.
